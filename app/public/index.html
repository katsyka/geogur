            GeoGur

            A precise tool for learning the world. Start from what you can see on a street, such as a shop, a tram stop and a church, and find every real place where those things stand together.

            1 Locate live

            Co-occurrence search. Pick a region (country, city, continent or the visible map area) and 2–5 features: about 170 presets, any brand or name, or any raw OSM tag (key=value, key~regex, key!=value). Then set a range. Results export as CSV or GeoJSON.

            1. The rarest feature becomes the anchor, which keeps the search fast.
            2. OpenStreetMap is searched for anchors that have every other feature within range.
            3. Results are ranked by spread, the largest distance between any two of the matched features.
            4. Each place shows exact coordinates, pairwise distances, address and OSM IDs, plus Open in Google Maps.
            2 Clues live

            Reverse lookup: tick what you see (driving side, alphabet, special letters, a domain or phone code, plates, road lines, speed units) and the list of 131 countries shrinks. The clue library holds about 2,500 described examples in 13 categories (bollards, poles, bus and tram stops, stop signs, crossing signs, chevrons, railway crossings, traffic lights, plates, street signs, house numbers, post boxes). You can filter by their attributes, and each one links to the exact Street View panorama where it was seen.

            3 Sun live

            Sun bearing + sun height (or shadow length) + date → latitude band, drawn on the map. Uses exact solar geometry, with your measurement error carried through.

            Data

            FeaturesOpenStreetMap via the Overpass API (live). Results are cached on the server.
            AddressesNominatim reverse geocoding, looked up when you open a result.
            Verification“Open in Google Maps” at exact coordinates (6 decimals ≈ 0.1 m).
            NextAn own pre-built index (PostGIS / H3) for fast searches across the whole world.
